Company story
Founding Story
Govardhan and Jaya developed delicious vegan and vegetarian recipes. They believed strongly in the power of fresh, whole foods. In fact, every meal was prepared fresh. Milk came front the family cows, and Govardhan would personally cut grass so that they too would eat only the freshest food. The family began harvesting their own turmeric, carefully seeking out the most aromatic herbs and spices to mill. Building his own custom mill. Govardhan began his spices business in the 1960s. Here he developed the family’s proprietary virtually heat-free spice processing for incredible flavour. Their custom rice mill milled perfect rice. As word of their extraordinary products spread, farmers from surrounding towns came to buy them by the bagful. In Indian culture recipes are handed down from mother to daughters and daughter-in-laws. An avid home chef, inspired by a legacy of delicious cuisines, Jaya helped create many of the Indianlife dishes now beloved throughout North America. She also encouraged young Rakesh to pursue his love of cooking and develop the recipes that have made Indianlife famous. Today Indianlife proudly carries on the Raniga family tradition of delicious, fresh and healthy natural vegetarian foods.
Company Mission
We’re passionate about delicious foods that are good for you, and good for the earth. We believe that when we bring healthy and delicious vegetarian and vegan foods to you, we are part of a “wheel” of good karma that benefits people and planet. Freshness is our mantra, and our family legacy. It’s also our pledge to you. So only the sweetest, most aromatic spices and freshest ingredients grace our recipes. Only the purest whole grains become our wraps and breads. We also believe that what’s not in our food is as important as what’s inside. So no artificial colors, preservatives or additives are used in our all natural, Non-GMO and organic foods. We feel life’s real rewards are the people we meet along the way. So we source our raw ingredients from producers that share our commitment to sustainability. We support programs that reduce hunger and build strong communities. And we love the smiles we see when our family recipes become your family recipes.

Certifications

Certified production practices are those mandated by certification standards and enforced by 3rd party certification verifiers. Violating a practice removes a company's eligibility for the certification. Thus, a company is guaranteed to follow them if they received the corresponding certification (assuming no corruption). These practices are distinguished from production practices implied by the certification’s terminology (i.e. “organic”), but aren’t formally enforced. For example, poor soil management on an organic certified farm.

Regenerative Farming

The definition of “Regenerative management practices” that’s used to classify a farm’s management as regenerative or not, is based on the below excerpt from renowned regenerative farmer pioneer, Gabe Brown. Based on the company’s stated farming practices, a comparison is made to the ideal of regenerative management of a farm as outlined by the principles in this quote. A farm is deemed a practitioner of regenerative management of a farm, or not, to the extent its stated practices show evidence that its current operations are in accordance with these farm management principles, or there’s explicit evidence (through statements made) that the farm holds these farming management principles as an ideal, and is striving to improve its farming management practices towards them on a continuous basis.

Gabe Brown: “In the everyday work of my farm, most of the decisions I make, in one way or another, are driven by the goal of continuing to grow and protect soil. I follow five principles that were developed by nature, over eons of time. They are the same any place in the world where the sun shines and plants grow. Gardeners, farmers, and ranchers around the world are using these principles to grow nutrient-rich, deep topsoil with healthy watersheds.”

The five principles of soil health are:

  • Limited disturbance. Limit mechanical, chemical, and physical disturbance of soil.
  • Armor. Keep soil covered at all times.
  • Diversity. Strive for diversity of both plant and animal species.
  • Living roots. Maintain a living root in soil as long as possible throughout the year.
  • Integrated animals. Nature does not function without animals.
