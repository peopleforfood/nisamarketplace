Certifications

Certified production practices are those mandated by certification standards and enforced by 3rd party certification verifiers. Violating a practice removes a company's eligibility for the certification. Thus, a company is guaranteed to follow them if they received the corresponding certification (assuming no corruption). These practices are distinguished from production practices implied by the certification’s terminology (i.e. “organic”), but aren’t formally enforced. For example, poor soil management on an organic certified farm.

Regenerative Farming

The definition of “Regenerative management practices” that’s used to classify a farm’s management as regenerative or not, is based on the below excerpt from renowned regenerative farmer pioneer, Gabe Brown. Based on the company’s stated farming practices, a comparison is made to the ideal of regenerative management of a farm as outlined by the principles in this quote. A farm is deemed a practitioner of regenerative management of a farm, or not, to the extent its stated practices show evidence that its current operations are in accordance with these farm management principles, or there’s explicit evidence (through statements made) that the farm holds these farming management principles as an ideal, and is striving to improve its farming management practices towards them on a continuous basis.

Gabe Brown: “In the everyday work of my farm, most of the decisions I make, in one way or another, are driven by the goal of continuing to grow and protect soil. I follow five principles that were developed by nature, over eons of time. They are the same any place in the world where the sun shines and plants grow. Gardeners, farmers, and ranchers around the world are using these principles to grow nutrient-rich, deep topsoil with healthy watersheds.”

The five principles of soil health are:

  • Limited disturbance. Limit mechanical, chemical, and physical disturbance of soil.
  • Armor. Keep soil covered at all times.
  • Diversity. Strive for diversity of both plant and animal species.
  • Living roots. Maintain a living root in soil as long as possible throughout the year.
  • Integrated animals. Nature does not function without animals.
