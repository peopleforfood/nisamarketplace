How the farm is run

Regenerative Farming

A farm is considered “regenerative” if it strives towards the goals of • restoring the ecosystem the food production occurs on to a healthier state • imitating the diversity and health of the natural ecosystem it inhabits

Multiple farms can produce different crops or raise different animals, employ varying farming practices, and still all be considered regenerative because what they share in common is that their practices are aimed towards the 2 goals above. The needs of any piece of land are dependent on the unique characteristics of the ecosystem, and a technique that regenerates one ecosystem may not regenerate another. As such, the techniques of a regenerative farm must be considered in light of the needs that its ecosystem requires to be healthy.

With the above said, there are universal principles that need to be respected for any ecosystem to have long-term health, regardless of where it is on the Earth. The first universal principle that any farm striving towards ecological health must prioritize is soil health. The second universal principle is diversity: diversity of plants and of wildlife.

For a farm to be considered regenerative, it must provide specific examples of practices it employs that demonstrate commitment to both of these principles. If there are • statements made on the website that demonstrate commitment to the 2 goals of regenerative farming, • and there is evidence of specific practices that support the 2 universal principles of a healthy ecosystem

Then the farm is considered to be regenerative.

Below are listed specific examples that demonstrate each principle in practice.

Soil health • The farm does not till • The farm employs cover cropping • The farm plants diverse mixes of crops rather than monocultures • The farm thoughtfully pairs crops • The farm doesn’t use synthetic inputs (synthetic fertilizer, herbicides, fungicides, pesticides) • The farm reuses organic matter like dead plant matter and/or animal manure instead of relying on synthetic fertilizer • The farm avoids having bare soil any part of the year • The farm has living roots in the soil year-round

Diversity of plants and wildlife: • The farm integrates grazing animals • The farm provides a home and habitat for not only farm animals but also pollinators, predator insects, earthworms, and all of the microbiology that drive ecosystem function • The farm maintains a garden or an orchard • The farm grows plants other than what they harvest for sale • The farm plants a diversity of grasses, forbs, legumes, and shrubs. • The farm plants a diversity of plants with these characteristics: shallow roots, some deep, some fibrous, some tap, high-carbon, low-carbon.
